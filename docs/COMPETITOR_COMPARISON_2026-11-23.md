**23 November 2026 · Codenest (codenest.uk, `master` @ b9a0f57) against three UK paid-search
competitors**

The three sites are the ones the owner reached through Google Ads for "fractional CTO" in the UK:

| Site | Page reviewed | Who runs it | Base |
|---|---|---|---|
| S30 Labs | `s30labs.io/fractional-cto/` | Max Prychyna, ex-Booking.com engineering lead | Netherlands, takes UK clients |
| PIMASI | `pimasi.com/` and `/fractional-cto` | Simon Piscitelli, ex-PrimaryBid, Keyzy, LSEG | England |
| Codeidon | `codeidon.com/l/fractional-cto` (ad landing page) | Süleyman Onur Kara | UK and Netherlands |

**Method.** Each page was fetched from its clean URL, with the ad tracking parameters removed, and
compared as rendered HTML text. Content that loads only with JavaScript was not seen; this affects
part of Codeidon's "90-day roadmap" and engagement-model sections. All three competitors are
CTO-only and run by one person. Codenest's CFO seat has no competitor in this set, so this compares
the **CTO journey** only. Competitor copy is paraphrased rather than quoted.

---

## Summary

All three competitors do four things that Codenest does not:

1. a **defined first step**, beyond the free call;
2. **proof written as decisions**, not as task lists;
3. a **direct booking link**;
4. a **plain, literal h1**.

PIMASI is the strongest of the three. S30 is the closest to Codenest's CTO buyer: startups without
a CTO, and non-technical founders managing an agency. Codeidon is the weakest. Its price-led copy
and heavy form show what Codenest should avoid.

Codenest leads on things none of them can match: **a second seat (CFO)**, **a real agentic AI
build**, **regulated enterprise pedigree**, and **content depth** (seven guides, 20 posts, a
runway calculator). Its CTO page does not convert as well as theirs. The gap is in the first step,
the proof and the booking, not in credentials.

## What they do better, and what Codenest should take from it

Each item names the pattern, not the wording. Nothing here should be copied.

1. **A paid, productised first step (PIMASI). This is the biggest single gap.** A fixed-fee,
   two-week review with named deliverables gives a cautious founder a small first purchase. It also
   makes the fee for the full engagement easier to justify. *For Codenest:* the review already
   proposes a fixed-scope **"Raise readiness"** offer (both seats) and **technical diligence**
   (CTO). Make one of them the default first step after the call, and credit its fee against a
   retainer. Agreeing the fee on the call keeps the 4 Aug no-published-prices rule.

2. **Proof written as decisions (S30).** S30's strongest section explains decisions and what each
   one cost. That shows the judgement a CTO is hired for, not the tasks done. *For Codenest:* the
   review already says the Opayo and AstraZeneca studies read as contracting. Rewrite each study
   around **two or three decisions Ankit owned**: the choice, the alternative rejected, the cost
   and what it saved. Regeno lends itself to this now, without waiting for outcome numbers. For
   example: why a company brain first, why sandbox per run, why merge gates.

3. **Direct booking (S30, PIMASI).** Both put a calendar link next to every CTA. Codenest's form
   leads to an email exchange about times. *For Codenest:* add a scheduler, then rename the CTA.
   This amends BRANDING §13.3, which keeps "Request a Strategy Call" only "until a real scheduler
   ships".

4. **A literal h1 (S30).** S30's h1 says what you are buying and when you stop buying it. Codenest's
   says neither. *For Codenest:* the review's proposed h1 still applies. A version that also states
   the exit: *"A Fractional CTO and a Fractional CFO for pre-seed and seed founders, until you can
   hire full-time."*

5. **Stated capacity (S30).** A true client limit is a credible quality signal and a reason to book
   now. *For Codenest:* state the per-seat client limit if there is one [owner to supply]. Do not
   invent one.

6. **Technical diligence and hiring as named offers (S30, PIMASI).** Both sell diligence to
   *investors and acquirers*, not only to founders preparing for it. That is a second buyer who
   refers founders afterwards. *For Codenest:* a diligence offer with a written report fits the
   existing `/refer` programme aimed at VCs and accelerators.

7. **Search terms (PIMASI, S30).** PIMASI's footer names part-time CTO, CTO as a service and interim
   CTO outright. Codenest uses none of these terms (review section 9). *For Codenest:* one FAQ on the
   CTO page that uses all three naturally, not a keyword footer.

## What Codenest should not take

- **Codeidon's form:** phone, website and a budget band are all required before a first call. It
  screens out the pre-seed founders Codenest wants. Keep Codenest's four-field form.
- **Price-led framing:** Codeidon opens on avoiding a full-time salary of £180k+. It is the most
  generic page of the three, and it shows why the review recommends moving Codenest's "Save 60-80%"
  out of the hero.
- **"Every mode from one person" (PIMASI):** this works for a 10-50 person software business. For
  pre-seed founders it reads as an agency, and Codenest's review already warns against diluting the
  seat into delivery.

## Where Codenest is ahead (keep and lead with it)

- **Two seats.** No competitor here offers finance. The "both seats" moments (before a raise, a
  build-versus-buy decision, 0-to-1) are uncontested in this set.
- **A real agentic AI build** with a current client. Only PIMASI mentions AI on its homepage, and
  only as automation and writing.
- **Regulated pedigree:** the Ministry of Justice, Tesco Bank, HSBC, AstraZeneca and seven years at
  Opayo. S30's Booking.com and PIMASI's LSEG are comparable; Codeidon has no named employers.
- **Content:** guides, comparisons, cost posts and a runway calculator. PIMASI has a blog; S30 and
  Codeidon have none on these pages.
- **Honesty devices:** "When we are the wrong call" matches S30's not-a-fit list and goes further
  than PIMASI and Codeidon.

## Changes to the 23 Nov review

- **Pricing (review section 6 and the P1 row).** None of the three competitors publishes a price, so
  Codenest's 4 Aug no-published-prices rule matches the market. The review's recommendation to
  revisit it is **downgraded from P1 to P3**. The fixed-fee first step (item 1 above) does more
  of the pre-qualification job without a price list.
- **New P1: a productised first step.** A fixed-fee "Raise readiness" or diligence review, with the
  fee credited against the retainer.
- **Raised to P1: proof written as decisions.** Merges with the existing case-study row.
